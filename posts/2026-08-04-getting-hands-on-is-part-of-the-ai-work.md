In a Copilot for Excel meeting today, someone asked a good question.

Would it be easier to manually fix the formatting, like adding percentage signs where they were missing?

Or would it be better to keep prompting until the model got the result exactly right?

That sounds like a tiny workflow decision.

I do not think it is tiny.

I think it gets at something important about how these tools are supposed to fit into real work.

## The wrong goal is "never touch it"

There is a tempting fantasy around AI tools:

- write one perfect prompt
- get one perfect output
- never touch the work yourself

That fantasy is neat.
It is also not how most good work happens.

Sometimes another prompt is obviously the right move.
If the structure is wrong, the logic is wrong, or the model misunderstood the task, you should absolutely push the work back through the tool instead of cleaning up a bad result by hand.

But sometimes the work is already basically correct.
The table is useful.
The analysis is right.
The output is 90 percent there.

At that point, insisting on prompt purity can become its own kind of inefficiency.

If the only thing left is a little formatting cleanup, it may actually be better to open the file, touch the cells, add the percentage signs, and move on with your life.

## Hands-on does not mean failure

I think people sometimes treat manual intervention as if it proves the AI workflow failed.

I think that is backwards.

Getting hands-on with the output is often part of the quality control.

It is how you:

- notice whether the result actually makes sense
- catch subtle weirdness the model did not understand
- make judgment calls that are too small or contextual to deserve another full generation pass
- stay connected to the real artifact instead of only the prompt

That last part matters a lot.

If you only ever interact with AI work through prompts, it becomes very easy to stop really seeing the result.
You start managing the machine instead of examining the work.

That is one of the fastest paths to slop.

## A little friction is sometimes the healthy part

One thing I keep noticing with AI tools is that they remove friction so aggressively that you can lose the benefits friction used to provide.

Old friction was not always bad.
Sometimes it was the thing that forced you to look closely.

When a person has to manually fix a handful of spreadsheet cells, rewrite a heading, trim a paragraph, or adjust a chart label, they are not just "doing what the AI should have done."

They are re-entering the work.

They are checking:

- what the tool actually produced
- whether the result still matches the intent
- whether the output feels trustworthy enough to keep

That is not waste.
That is part of the process.

## The better workflow question

So the real question is not:

"Should I ever manually clean up AI output?"

The real question is:

"Where is the boundary between re-prompting and editing?"

I think the answer looks something like this:

- if the model got the task wrong, re-prompt
- if the structure is wrong, re-prompt
- if the logic is wrong, re-prompt
- if the output is basically right and only needs human finish work, touch it yourself

That is not anti-AI.
That is just a healthier division of labor.

The tool gets you most of the way there.
You provide the last-mile judgment.

## Touching the work keeps taste alive

Part of what people are worried about when they talk about AI slop is not just correctness.

It is deadness.

Outputs that are technically acceptable but visibly unloved.
Work that nobody really inspected.
Results that were generated, accepted, and shipped without anyone putting their hands on them.

I do not think the solution is to reject AI assistance.

I think the solution is to stay in contact with the artifact.

Read the spreadsheet.
Fix the label.
Adjust the sentence.
Move the chart.
Question the summary.

Even if it is slightly slower.

Especially if it is slightly slower.

That little bit of contact may be the thing that keeps the process honest.

## The point is not prompt purity

I do not want a workflow where the human is reduced to prompt vending.

I want a workflow where the tool accelerates the boring parts, but the person still applies judgment, taste, and responsibility at the end.

Sometimes that means writing a better prompt.

Sometimes that means changing the percentage signs yourself.

The goal is not to avoid touching the work.

The goal is to get to good work faster without mentally checking out along the way.
